Executive Summary

This analysis compares Domain Partners (EAW, Foundever, Highspring, JDA, Magnit) against Intuit across two products: TTL Full Service Consumer (FS) and TTL Assisted Consumer (TTLA). Each metric is computed as sum(numerator) / sum(denominator) — a volume-weighted approach that reflects actual workload rather than simple averages.

Partners analyzed: EAW (EDUCATION AT WORK), Foundever (FOUNDEVER), Highspring (HIGHSPRING LLC), JDA (JDA), Magnit (MAGNIT). Intuit rows include all Intuit-sourced experts. Foundever appears in FS only; EAW, Highspring, JDA, and Magnit serve both products.

FS Performance Story

On Full Service, Domain Partners collectively deliver stronger customer satisfaction and operational efficiency than Intuit. Partners Total tNPS of 75.61 exceeds Intuit's 69.48 (+6.14 points), based on 14,684 partner surveys. Handled Conversion (HC) and Customer Service Time (CST) also favor partners — suggesting partners convert engagements effectively while spending less time per return. However, partners handle a broadly comparable SKU mix, which affects blended CST comparisons.

TTLA Performance Story

TTL Assisted tells a different story. Intuit outperforms partners on tNPS, Issue Resolution (IR), and Service Quality Score (SQS), while AHT is roughly comparable. Partners Total tNPS of 65.08 trails Intuit's 73.37 by 8.28 points across 89,811 surveys. JDA dominates partner TTLA volume (~79%), so aggregate partner metrics are heavily influenced by JDA's performance profile.

Methodology Note: All metrics are computed as weighted averages: sum(numerator) / sum(denominator) across all rows matching the filter criteria. Mix-adjusted metrics reweight Partners Total to Intuit's SKU and customer-type workload mix. tNPS and IR are calculated as (numerator/denominator) × 100. AHT = total handle time / total contacts (minutes). CST = total service time / total engagements. HC = handled conversions / total handled. SQS = quality score numerator / denominator × 100. Higher is better for tNPS, IR, SQS, and HC. Lower is better for AHT and CST.
