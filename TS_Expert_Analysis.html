Analysis: Is Hiring New PL1 Tax Specialists Valuable?

FS — Tax Specialists Underperform

  • PL1 New Hires show the widest gap: TS tNPS of 55.72 trails Non-TS by 15.93 points. HC (47.31 vs 65.59) and IR (59.06 vs 60.83) are also lower. CST is higher (2.82 vs 2.26).
  • The gap persists at PL1 and PL2 (end of season): TS is below Non-TS in tNPS, IR, and HC at these levels. TS doesn't begin to close the gap until PL3 (end of season), where tNPS flips to 76.51 vs 66.61.
  • Growth trajectory is visible (beginning-of-season view): Experts who started the season at PL1 underperform, but those who started at PL2 already outperform Non-TS in tNPS (73.92 vs 67.62) and CST (2.45 vs 2.59). This suggests TS performance improves substantially with experience.
  • SKU mix is a major confounder: TS handles 49.3% Basic (vs 28.1% for Non-TS) and only 18.0% Premium (vs 43.3%). Since Basic returns typically yield higher tNPS, the SKU allocation may be masking some TS capability. When comparing tNPS within the same SKU at PL3, TS outperforms Non-TS on Basic (84.93 vs 75.40) and Deluxe (77.91 vs 72.81), and closes the gap on Premium (71.18 vs 54.84).
  • CST is the one consistent FS strength: TS has lower overall CST (2.57 vs 2.96), driven partly by SKU mix but also by genuine efficiency at higher PLs — PL4 TS achieves the lowest per-SKU CST across all tiers (Basic: 1.52 vs 1.81, Deluxe: 1.96 vs 2.49, Premium: 3.16 vs 4.12).

TTLA — Tax Specialists Consistently Outperform

  • TS outperforms Non-TS at every proficiency level in tNPS, IR, and SQS — both at the beginning and end of season.
  • PL1 New Hire TTLA: TS tNPS of 68.78 beats Non-TS 61.48 (+7.30). IR (87.18 vs 82.80) and SQS (81.93 vs 79.82) also higher.
  • The advantage is strongest at PL1 (end of season): TS tNPS of 68.40 vs Non-TS 50.96 — a +17.4 point advantage. This suggests TS training or selection provides a meaningful head start in customer satisfaction for TTLA.
  • AHT is the only trade-off: TS AHT is slightly higher overall (23.67 vs 22.41 min), but this gap narrows and reverses at PL4 (18.36 vs 22.36 min). Even at PL1, the AHT difference is minimal (24.40 vs 24.17).
  • SKU-level analysis confirms the advantage: TS outperforms Non-TS in tNPS across all three SKUs (Basic: 77.25 vs 70.72, Deluxe: 76.22 vs 72.16, Premium: 69.37 vs 67.17). This is not a SKU-mix artifact — it is a genuine quality advantage.

Summary Assessment

  • FS value is limited at PL1–PL2 but grows significantly by PL3, where TS begins to outperform in tNPS, SQS, and CST. The investment in TS experience pays off over time for FS.
  • TTLA value is immediate and sustained. Even PL1 New Hire Tax Specialists outperform their Non-TS counterparts on all quality metrics. Given that 88% of TS experts handle both FS and TTLA, the TTLA quality lift applies to most new hires.
  • The combined product picture matters: While FS-only performance suggests PL1 TS underperforms, the majority of these same experts are simultaneously delivering superior TTLA results. A holistic view across both products shows net positive value, particularly as experts gain experience.

Purpose: The raw comparison between Tax Specialists and Non-TS is confounded by differences in SKU mix and customer type (New vs Returning). This analysis reweights TS metrics using Non-TS's volume distribution to isolate the true performance gap from composition effects.

Methodology

For each metric, performance is computed in granular cells defined by SKU (Basic / Deluxe / Premium) × Customer Type (New / Returning) — yielding 6 cells. The mix-adjusted TS value applies Non-TS's volume weights to TS's per-cell performance, answering: "What would TS's metric be if they had the same SKU and customer mix as Non-TS?"

For IR and HC, SKU-level denominators are not available, so adjustment is by Customer Type only (2 cells). tNPS, CST, and AHT use the full 6-cell adjustment.

Interpretation Guide

  • Mix Effect = Mix-Adjusted TS − Actual TS. A positive value for tNPS/IR/HC means TS was benefiting from a favorable mix; a negative CST/AHT mix effect means TS's mix was making them look better.
  • Adjusted Gap = Mix-Adjusted TS − Non-TS. This is the "true" performance difference after removing composition effects.
  • For CST and AHT (lower is better): a positive adjusted gap means TS is worse after adjustment; a negative gap means TS is better.
  • This analysis adjusts for SKU mix and New/Returning customer mix only. Other confounders (partner mix, proficiency level distribution, volume per expert) are not adjusted here.
